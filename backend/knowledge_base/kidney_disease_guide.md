# Clinical Reference Guide: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) & Renal Function

## 1. Overview & Disease Description
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is characterized by persistent structural or functional abnormalities of the kidneys present for > 3 months, with implications for health. The disease typically manifests as a gradual and progressive decline in the glomerular filtration rate (GFR), leading to impaired clearance of metabolic waste products, electrolyte disturbances, fluid overload, and systemic complications.

### Staging by KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes):
- **Stage 1**: Normal or high GFR (>= 90 mL/min/1.73 m2) with persistent kidney damage (proteinuria/albuminuria).
- **Stage 2**: Mild GFR reduction (60–89 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- **Stage 3a / 3b**: Moderate GFR reduction (45–59 and 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- **Stage 4**: Severe GFR reduction (15–29 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- **Stage 5**: Kidney Failure / End-Stage Renal Disease (< 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 or dialysis dependence).

## 2. Key Clinical Biomarkers & Risk Factors

### Serum Creatinine (sc) & Blood Urea Nitrogen (bu)
- **Serum Creatinine**: Normal 0.6–1.2 mg/dL for men, 0.5–1.1 mg/dL for women. Creatinine is a breakdown product of muscle creatine phosphate; elevated levels reflect diminished glomerular filtration capacity.
- **Blood Urea / BUN**: Normal 7–20 mg/dL. Elevated blood urea (azotemia) reflects uremic toxin retention and reduced renal clearance.

### Urine Albumin (al) & Specific Gravity (sg)
- **Albuminuria Rating (0 to 5)**: Albumin is normally retained by the glomerular basement membrane. Leakage of albumin indicates glomerular filtration barrier disruption (e.g. from diabetic glomerulosclerosis or hypertensive nephrosclerosis).
- **Specific Gravity (sg)**: Normal 1.015–1.025. Low or fixed specific gravity (isosthenuria ~1.010) indicates tubular loss of concentrating and diluting ability.

### Hematology: Hemoglobin (hemo) & Packed Cell Volume (pcv)
- **Normocytic Normochromic Anemia**: Hemoglobin < 13.0 g/dL (men) or < 12.0 g/dL (women).
- **Pathophysiology**: Diseased peritubular interstitial cells fail to produce sufficient erythropoietin (EPO), resulting in progressive renal anemia.

### Electrolytes: Serum Sodium (sod) & Potassium (pot)
- **Sodium**: Normal 135–145 mEq/L. Dysregulation reflects impaired free water excretion or hypervolemic hyponatremia.
- **Potassium**: Normal 3.5–5.0 mEq/L. Impaired distal tubular potassium excretion can cause life-threatening hyperkalemia (> 5.5 mEq/L) leading to cardiac dysrhythmias.

### Comorbid Drivers: Hypertension (htn) & Diabetes Mellitus (dm)
- Diabetes and hypertension account for > 70% of all end-stage renal disease cases.
- Glomerular hyperfiltration followed by podocyte effacement drives progressive nephron sclerosis.

## 3. Prevention & Evidence-Based Renal Protection Recommendations
According to KDIGO Clinical Practice Guidelines:
1. **Renoprotective Blood Pressure Management**:
   - Target blood pressure < 120 mmHg systolic using standardized office readings when tolerated.
   - First-line pharmacotherapy with ACE inhibitors (ACEi) or Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs) for patients with albuminuria.
2. **Dietary Modifications**:
   - Protein intake moderation: 0.8 g/kg body weight/day for non-dialysis CKD Stages 3–5.
   - Dietary sodium restriction to < 2.0 g/day (< 5.0 g sodium chloride) to control blood pressure and volume overload.
   - Potassium and phosphorus monitoring in advanced stages.
3. **Glycemic & Lifestyle Control**:
   - Optimization of glycemic control in diabetic kidney disease with SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists.
   - Avoidance of nephrotoxic medications (e.g. non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs / NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, iodinated radiocontrast without hydration).
