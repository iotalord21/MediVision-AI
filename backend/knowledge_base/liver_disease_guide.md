# Clinical Reference Guide: Liver Disease & Hepatic Function Evaluation

## 1. Overview & Disease Description
Liver disease encompasses a wide spectrum of acute and chronic pathological conditions affecting hepatic parenchyma and biliary architecture. Causes include Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease / Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD/NAFLD), alcoholic liver disease, viral hepatitis (B and C), autoimmune hepatitis, drug-induced liver injury (DILI), and toxic cholestasis. Left unchecked, chronic inflammation progresses to bridging fibrosis, cirrhosis, and portal hypertension.

### Major Patterns of Hepatic Injury:
- **Hepatocellular Pattern**: Characterized by marked elevations in aminotransferases (SGPT/ALT and SGOT/AST), reflecting hepatocyte necrosis or membrane leakage.
- **Cholestatic Pattern**: Characterized by prominent elevations in Alkaline Phosphatase (AlkPhos) and Total/Direct Bilirubin, reflecting impaired bile synthesis or biliary obstruction.
- **Infiltrative / Synthetic Dysfunction Pattern**: Characterized by hypoalbuminemia, prolonged prothrombin time, and altered Albumin/Globulin ratio.

## 2. Key Clinical Biomarkers & Risk Factors

### Serum Aminotransferases: SGPT (ALT) & SGOT (AST)
- **SGPT / ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)**: Normal reference range 7–56 IU/L. Primarily concentrated in hepatocytes; the most specific enzymatic marker of acute or chronic liver parenchymal damage.
- **SGOT / AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)**: Normal reference range 10–40 IU/L. Present in liver, cardiac muscle, skeletal muscle, and kidneys.
- **De Ritis Ratio (AST/ALT Ratio)**: An AST/ALT ratio > 2.0 strongly suggests alcoholic liver disease, whereas AST/ALT < 1.0 is typical of MASLD/NAFLD and acute viral hepatitis.

### Alkaline Phosphatase (AlkPhos) & Biliary Markers
- **Alkaline Phosphatase**: Normal range 44–147 IU/L. Synthesized by biliary canalicular membranes and bone osteoblasts. Elevations indicate intrahepatic cholestasis, biliary ductular inflammation, or extrahepatic biliary tract obstruction.

### Total & Direct Bilirubin (tot_bilirubin, direct_bilirubin)
- **Total Bilirubin**: Normal range 0.2–1.2 mg/dL.
- **Direct (Conjugated) Bilirubin**: Normal range 0.0–0.3 mg/dL.
- **Clinical Significance**: Hyperbilirubinemia produces visible jaundice/icterus when total bilirubin exceeds 2.5–3.0 mg/dL. Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia reflects impaired hepatocellular excretion into bile or biliary tree occlusion.

### Serum Proteins: Albumin & Albumin/Globulin Ratio (ag_ratio)
- **Albumin**: Normal range 3.5–5.0 g/dL. Exclusively synthesized by the liver with a half-life of 20 days. Progressive hypoalbuminemia indicates chronic synthetic hepatic failure or severe malnutrition.
- **A/G Ratio**: Normal 1.0–2.2. A reduced or reversed A/G ratio (< 1.0) frequently occurs in cirrhosis due to decreased albumin synthesis paired with hypergammaglobulinemia from reticuloendothelial stimulation.

## 3. Prevention & Evidence-Based Hepatic Care Recommendations
According to the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD):
1. **Lifestyle & Metabolic Intervention**:
   - For MASLD/NAFLD: Weight loss of 7–10% through a hypocaloric diet reduces hepatic steatosis, necroinflammation, and fibrosis.
   - Mediterranean diet emphasizing monounsaturated fats, leafy vegetables, lean proteins, and avoidance of high-fructose corn syrup.
2. **Toxin & Alcohol Avoidance**:
   - Complete alcohol abstinence for patients with abnormal liver biochemistry or diagnosed fatty liver / cirrhosis.
   - Caution with hepatotoxic agents, paracetamol/acetaminophen overuse (> 2 g/day in cirrhosis), and unverified herbal supplements.
3. **Screening & Vaccination**:
   - Hepatitis A and B vaccination for susceptible patients with chronic liver disease.
   - Periodic ultrasound and elastography monitoring for fibrosis staging in high-risk individuals.
